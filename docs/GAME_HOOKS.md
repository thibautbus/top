# Game hooks

What the host hooks in the running game, what it reads there, where it may write, and the facts of the game's engine those rules rely on. The engine is described as oracles-disasm documents it, at the commit pinned in `config/disasm.json`; the symbol names are the disassembly's. Addresses are Ages US; Seasons shares the engine with the differences of section 7, and every address the host uses is resolved per game from the generated tables (`engine/game/guest/guest_tables_*.c`), never written by hand (`tools/check_guest_addresses.py` refuses one).

Conventions: `bb:aaaa` is CPU address `aaaa` in ROM bank `bb`; bank 0 functions are always visible, the others only while their bank is mapped at `$4000-$7fff`. WRAM variables are given by their CPU address; those prefixed `w2`, `w3`, `w4`, `w7` live in that WRAM bank, mapped at `$d000-$dfff` by SVBK; `w1` is bank 1, the objects'.

## 1. Memory and screen

WRAM bank 0 (`$c000-$cfff`) holds the sound, the stacks (`$c0b0-$c2bf`), the threads' states (`$c2e0`), the generic buffer `wBigBuffer` (`$c300`), the vblank function queue (`$c400`), the inputs and graphics registers, the objects to draw (`$c500`), the save file in RAM (`$c5b0-$c700`), the room flags (`$c700-$cb00`), the OAM in RAM (`$cb00`), text and menus, the game variables (`$cc00-$cdff`), the room's collisions (`$ce00`) and layout (`$cf00`). Bank 1 holds the objects (section 4.1); bank 2 palettes, the animation queue and the dungeon layout; bank 3 the current tileset's data (`w3TileMappingData`, 8 bytes per metatile, `w3TileCollisions`); bank 4 menus and the status bar; bank 7 the text box. HRAM holds among others the RNG (`hRng1`/`hRng2`), `hRomBank`, `hActiveThread`, `hCameraY`/`hCameraX` (16 bits each) and the object being updated (`hActiveObjectType`/`hActiveObject`).

The screen:

- two background maps, `$9800` and `$9c00`, used in turn for scrolling transitions; attributes in VRAM bank 1;
- 8x16 sprites, OAM copied by DMA from `wOam` at every vblank;
- the status bar takes the first 16 lines: the LCD interrupt on LYC switches LCDC/SCY/SCX/WY/WX between `wGfxRegs1` (top, status bar) and `wGfxRegs2` (the play area), so the play area is 160x128 pixels, 10x8 metatiles of 16x16;
- those lines show rows 30 and 31 of the `$9c00` map, which `checkReloadStatusBarGraphics` copies from `w4StatusBarTileMap` and `w4StatusBarAttributeMap` (two rows of 32 cells, 20 seen). `loadStatusBarMap` loads one of three maps there (`map_hud_normal`, `map_hud_extra_hearts`, `map_hud_biggoron_sword` and their `flg_hud_*` attributes): the normal one; the one tightened when `wLinkMaxHealth` exceeds fourteen hearts (`cp 14*4+1`), eight hearts a row where there are seven; Biggoron's sword on B, whatever the hearts. The bar is four blocks side by side whatever its map: B and A (pixels 0 to 79, 40 each; 0 to 71 tightened, 36 each; one frame with Biggoron's sword, as wide as the hearts leave it), the money (24 pixels: the rupee's icon, a dungeon's key, Seasons' ore in Subrosia, over three digits; `correctAddressForExtraHeart` moves it a cell left when tightened), the hearts (two rows of seven or eight cells, to the bar's right edge). The icons of B and A's items are OAM entries 0 to 3 over the first block. Each map signs itself by the cells of its frame no icon, count or heart is written over (row, column: tile, attributes): normal 0,4: `$06`,`$e0`; 1,4: `$06`,`$a0`; 0,9: `$06`,`$e0`; 1,9: `$06`,`$a0`; tightened 0,4: `$07`,`$c0`; 1,4: `$07`,`$80`; 0,8: `$08`,`$a0`; 1,8: `$06`,`$a0`; Biggoron's 0,1: `$05`,`$80`; 1,1: `$06`,`$80`; 0,7: `$08`,`$a0`; 1,7: `$06`,`$a0`; the three fan games keep the same maps at the same addresses. `hideStatusBar` clears the map (tiles 0, attributes `$80`) for the opening and closing cutscenes;
- `hLcdInterruptBehaviour`, copied from `hNextLcdInterruptBehaviour` at every vblank, has seven behaviours (`lcdInterrupt`, `bank0.s`): 0 and 1 load SCX or SCY from `wBigBuffer` at the start of every line, at index LY, without waiting for a hblank, and rearm LYC for the next line (underwater waves, the distortion of a strange force). The game stores there a table of 128 steps (`w2WaveScrollValues`) read modulo 128 line after line from a phase that moves with `wFrameCounter`, so the 128 lines of the play area are whole periods of the wave. Under water, `checkUpdateUnderwaterWaves` (Ages) fills the buffer in the logic for the next frame: line i takes `wGfxRegs2.SCX` + `w2WaveScrollValues[(SCY + wFrameCounter + i) & 127]` (a signed sine, its second half the first negated), the wave held to the background's rows, from the registers of the frame before: where the camera moves, the screen shows the waves off by its move, the lines the logic writes after the LCD has drawn them keeping the last frame's. 2, 3 and 4 are normal play, the status bar / play area switch at line 16; 5 (the ring menu) and 6 (the Onox fight) rearm LYC and rewrite registers several times a frame. All are register writes that a journal stamped with LY replays, which is what the native renderer does. Under water, the status bar's switch (behaviour 2, which waits for line 15's hblank, then becomes 0) hands line 16 to the waves: when its handler returns late, line 16's write falls while that line is drawn, and the PPU mixes the two values (the tiles already fetched keep the old scroll, the next ones take the new tile column with the fine scroll latched at the line's start). The journal does not say where in the line a write falls, so the renderer leaves such a line out of its comparison. The window is a layer of its own during menus and the inventory's slide;
- the game runs in CGB double speed.

## 2. Main loop, threads and the order of a frame

`_mainLoop` (`00:0933`): `pollInput`, then the four threads, then the dirty palettes, the copy of `wGfxRegs1` to `wGfxRegsFinal`, and a `halt` until vblank. One iteration is one frame of the game.

The threads are cooperative: a thread yields with `resumeThreadInAFrames` (`00:0900`) or `resumeThreadNextFrame` (`00:08fe`), which save SP in `wThreadStateBuffer` (`$c2e0`, 8 bytes per thread: state, countdown, SP, function). There is no preemption: a thread an interrupt stops resumes where it was. A suspended thread keeps on its stack, from its saved SP, BC, DE, HL and its resume address: those eight bytes are live state, and only the bytes strictly below the saved SP are dead. This is why every WRAM fingerprint the host compares excludes exactly the dead bytes below each thread's SP.

| Thread | Role | Stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | the intro (`introThreadStart`) and the game over screen | `wThread0Stack` |
| 1 | `mainThreadStart`: all of the gameplay, and the file select | `wThread1Stack` |
| 2 | text, through `textThreadStart` | `wThread2Stack` |
| 3 | `paletteFadeThreadStart`: palette fades | `wThread3Stack` |

`mainThreadStart` (`00:33a1`) loops on: `wPlaytimeCounter` and `wFrameCounter` incremented, `bank1.runGameLogic`, `drawAllSprites`, `checkReloadStatusBarGraphics`, `resumeThreadNextFrame`. `runGameLogic` dispatches on `wGameState` (`$c2ee`): 0 `initializeGame`, 1 `loadingRoom` (a room loaded from scratch, as after a warp), 2 `standardGameState`, which dispatches on `wCutsceneIndex` (`$c2ef`: 1 is normal play, 0 a room loading, the others cutscenes), 3 `linkSummonedCutscene`.

In normal play, `cutscene01` runs the menus (`updateMenus`, which holds the frame while a menu is open), then `updateAllObjects`, then, if bit 2 of `wScrollMode` is set (a transition decided in the same iteration), `getNextActiveRoom`, `wScrollMode = 8`, `loadTilesetAndRoomLayout`, `loadRoomCollisions` and `initializeRoom`; then the death checks.

`updateAllObjects` (`00:345b`) runs, in this order: the special objects (the companion, then Link); `updateItems`; `setEnemyTargetToLinkPosition`, then `updateEnemies`; `updateParts`; `updateInteractions`; `bank1.func_4000`, the screen transitions' state machine (section 3); the ridden and carried objects; `loadLinkAndCompanionAnimationFrame`; `updateItemsPost` and the object that follows Link; `updateCamera`; `updateChangedTileQueue`; `updateAnimations` (section 5). Then `drawAllSprites` builds `wOam`. Between `updateAnimations` and `drawAllSprites`, or just after, positions, layout, animations and palettes are consistent: that is where the host reads the state of a frame.

Interrupts: the **vblank** handler (`00:09f8`) writes LCDC/SCY/SCX/WY/WX/LYC from `wGfxRegsFinal` and, if the main loop is waiting, copies `wGfxRegs2` to `wGfxRegs3`, runs the whole `wVBlankFunctionQueue` (graphics DMA, tile rows during transitions), `updateDirtyPalettes` and the OAM DMA; on a frame the main loop did not finish, nothing is committed and the PPU shows the previous frame again. The **LCD** handler switches at the status bar and serves behaviours 0 and 1; the **timer** drives the sound. Neither the vblank nor the LCD handler calls gameplay code: a hook on a gameplay function is never re-entered by an interrupt.

## 3. Screen transitions and scrolling

For a small room, `hCameraY`/`hCameraX` are 0: the screen is the room. For a large room, `updateCameraPosition` aims at the followed object minus half a screen, bounded to the room, and moves one pixel a frame towards it; `updateGfxRegs2Scroll` derives SCX/SCY of `wGfxRegs2` from it, plus `wScreenOffsetX/Y` and the screen shake. The game copies its camera to the display registers at the vblank that opens the next frame, after the logic that moved it: the image on screen was drawn with the camera one frame older.

`wScrollMode` (`$cd00`) governs what updates:

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `$00` | a warp in progress (`initiateWarp`) |
| `$01` | normal play: tile animations, camera and text active |
| `$02` | loading or a non-scrolling entry (`loadingRoom`, `screenTransitionState1`): enemies frozen |
| `$04` | a scrolling transition decided by `screenTransitionState2`; `cutscene01` calls `getNextActiveRoom` in the same iteration and sets `$08` before `initializeRoom` |
| `$08` | the entered room loading, then the scroll (set back to `$01` at the end by `setInstrumentsDisabledCounterAndScrollMode`): enemies, interactions and parts frozen |

The state machine is `bank1.func_400b`, on `wScreenTransitionState` (`$cd04`):

- state 0: entry into a room from scratch, then `initializeRoomBoundaryAndLoadAnimations`, which sets the room's size (20x16 or 30x22 tiles), the transition boundaries, the camera limits and the tile animation;
- state 1: after a warp, the screen opens in 32 steps, each writing a column of the new room into the other background map through the vblank queue and moving the camera; it starts from the middle column of the window and writes one column a frame, alternately left and right. A column not written yet shows the blank tile: the curtain that opens from the centre;
- state 2: normal play; each frame, if Link's y is at most 5 the transition goes up, past the bottom boundary down, and the same in x; bit 7 of `wScreenTransitionDirection` forces a transition without any test of position or collision;
- states 3 to 5: the transition, Link in `LINK_STATE_WARPING` (`$0a`), the new room from `getNextActiveRoom`, its layout loaded, then the scroll. `cutscene01` writes `wScrollMode` 8 before `loadTilesetAndRoomLayout` and `loadRoomCollisions`, a load whose frames the vblank interrupts: until the frame of state 3 that loads them ends, `wRoomCollisions` holds the room left's collisions, or part of the room entered's. What that load reads is fixed once `wScrollMode` is 8: `getNextActiveRoom` and `loadTilesetData` have named the room and its tileset (`wActiveRoom`, `wTilesetLayout`, `wTilesetLayoutGroup`, `wActiveCollisions`, `wTilesetFlags`), and the substitutions read bytes the load leaves alone (the room's flags, the global flags, the portal; [`ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md`](ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md), section 17).

The scroll itself (state 5) sets `wScreenScrollCounter` (`$14` columns horizontally, `$10` rows vertically) and the step `wcd14` (±4 px); each step moves `wGfxRegs2` and `hCameraX/Y` by the step and Link by `$0060` in x or `$0080` in y (8.8 fixed point), and a column is drawn when the register is aligned on 8, so every other step. Then the unique graphics load one entry a frame, `wScrollMode` returns to 1, and `finishScrollingTransition` moves Link back by ±160 or ±128, resets `hCameraX/Y` and recomputes the camera. A horizontal crossing is forty steps of 4 px plus the preparation (state 3: layout, collisions and tile animation; state 4: unique graphics), during which `linkState01` skips Link's movement (`wScrollMode & $0e`): he is frozen, and the game moves him 15 px (16 vertically) in all. His walk is the stream of `specialObjectAnimationTable`, advanced by `animateLinkWalking`, which the transition suspends too.

A swimming Link is frozen the same way: `linkState01` returns before `linkApplyTileTypes`, `linkUpdateSwimming` and `linkUpdateDiving`, so his speed (`w1Link.speed`, a row of `objectSpeedTable` times five, `$20` in 8.8 a row along an axis), his angle, his momentum (`var35`, `counter1`), the dive counter (`counter2`) and his animation (`SWIM` `$0b` or `DIVE` `$0c`, advanced outside a transition by `specialObjectAnimate` every frame, standing still included) stay as they are until he arrives. With the flippers he swims at `SPEED_80` (`SPEED_e0` with the swimmer's ring), and a stroke of A raises `speedTmp` by 5 every 4 frames for 13 frames, then lowers it for 12 (`linkUpdateFlippersSpeed`): its state 0 alone tests A in `wGameKeysJustPressed`, so the presses of a stroke are read by nobody and A held gives one stroke; with Ages' mermaid suit (`var2f` bit 6) at `SPEED_120` (`SPEED_160` with the ring). With the suit, the sea floor (`linkState01`), the surface (`overworldSwimmingState3`) and the side views (`linkUpdateSwimming_sidescroll`) all move Link by `linkUpdateVelocity@mermaidSuit`, which speeds him up by strokes: its `ld a,(wGameKeysJustPressed)`, `and` the four directions, on a new press plays `SND_SPLASH` and sets `var3e` to 4, and each frame `var3e`, decremented, stays at or above zero (the press's and four more), `var12` is set to `$14` so that `func_5933` takes a step that frame: the speed 5 towards `speedTmp`, the angle one of 32 towards `wLinkAngle`, or 5 away with no direction held, angle and speed reset when it reaches zero. Once the stroke is spent, a step of 5 is lost when `var12`, counted up each frame, reaches `var13` (5, the mermaid row of `updateLinkSpeed_withParam`), the angle kept. On the sea floor `linkState01` moves Link whether a direction is held or not (`ld a,(wLinkAngle) ; rlca ; ld c,$02` after the suit's velocity, his position updated, his walk not animated): with none he glides on at his angle, his speed lost by those steps of 5 to a stop. A held direction thus gives one stroke, and taps released within a stroke lose speed: the player taps a direction for at least the stroke's five frames, at most nine frames apart. On Ages' sea floor (`TILESETFLAG_UNDERWATER`, groups 2 and 3) the game has him walk: `wLinkSwimmingState` stays zero, the walk's animation takes the mermaid variant, and he moves by `linkUpdateVelocity@mermaidSuit`. A current (`TILETYPE_*CURRENT`) pushes him at `SPEED_c0` and takes him across an edge without the direction held (`wcc92`, `@adjustLinkOnConveyor`). A water tile carries the collision `SPECIALCOLLISION_HOLE` (`$10`, that of holes and lava too); its type comes from `tileTypesTable`, through the list of `wActiveCollisions` (`lookupCollisionTable`). Over water, `@checkCanTransitionOverWater` wants the flippers, and in Ages refuses deep water without the mermaid suit.

`screenTransitionState2@transition` also wants the edge's direction held (`convertLinkAngleToDirectionButtons` on `wLinkAngle`, eight sectors of 45 degrees), except on a current (`wcc92`'s low bits). It tests the position after Link has moved and, past the threshold, puts him back on the edge itself before checking the rest (`@transitionUp` and the others write `yh` or `xh`, then call `@transition`), whether the transition starts or not: at the end of a frame, a Link pushed against the edge without the direction held stands a pixel short of the test (6 at the top and the left, the boundary itself at the bottom and the right). A transition forced by bit 7 skips that write and starts from where Link's move took him, up to a pixel further. When two edges pass at once, the second transition, the horizontal one, overwrites the first. A stroke of the flippers carries Link on in the direction he faces with no direction held.

Link's walls are the eight points of `calculateAdjacentWallsBitset` (`link.s`): `y-3` at `x-3` and `x+2` above him, `y+7` at the same below, `x-5` at `y` and `y+5` on the left, `x+4` at the same on the right, each read by `checkTileCollisionAt_allowHoles` in `wRoomCollisions`: a value under `$10` blocks the quarter of the tile the point falls in (bit 3 the top left, 2 the top right, 1 the bottom left, 0 the bottom right); a special value blocks the columns (`$10` to `$17`) or the rows (`$18` to `$1f`) its byte in `@specialCollisions` marks, two pixels a bit from bit 0 (`bitTable`), a bridge's rails but not the stairs that slow him (`$18`); holes, water and lava (`$10`) are no wall there.

After `updateAllObjects`, `cutscene01` calls `func_60e9` before `getNextActiveRoom`: a forced transition does not stop a warp tile under Link from starting `initiateWarp`, which replaces the scroll with a warp. `wDisableWarpTiles` inhibits that check and the edge check of `checkWarpsTopDown`.

Warps (doors, stairs, holes) take another path: `warpSources` per room, `warpDestinations` per group (`docs/ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md`, section 6), `setWarpDestVariables`, then `wGameState = 1` and `loadingRoom`. Ages' dive into the sea and the surfacing do not go through `warpSources`: `checkForUnderwaterTransition` (`link.s`) sets the warp itself, to the room of the same index two groups down (a dive: 0 to 2, 1 to 3) or up (surfacing), at Link's tile, with bit 7 on `wWarpDestGroup`, and `cutscene01` plays it at the next iteration. A dive starts in `linkUpdateDiving` over `TILEINDEX_DEEP_WATER`; a surfacing from a B press under water where `checkLinkCanSurface` allows it, never on a whirlpool.

## 4. Objects

### 4.1 Slots

WRAM bank 1 holds 16 slots of 256 bytes, `$d000` to `$df00`. Each slot holds four 64-byte objects:

| Offset | Type | Structure | Slots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `$00` | item or special object | `ItemStruct` / `SpecialObjectStruct` | Link `$d000`, companion `$d100`, parent items `$d200`-`$d500`, weapon `$d600`, dynamic items `$d700`-`$db00`, reserved `$dc00`-`$df00` |
| `$40` | interaction | `InteractionStruct` | reserved `$d040` and `$d140`; dynamic `$d240`-`$df40` |
| `$80` | enemy | `ObjectStruct` | `$d080`-`$df80` |
| `$c0` | part | `ObjectStruct` | `$d0c0`-`$dfc0` |

`hActiveObject` holds the slot's high byte (`$d0`-`$df`), `hActiveObjectType` the offset. The `object*` functions of bank 0 work on `de = hActiveObject:hActiveObjectType`. An object's mode is bits 0-1 of `enabled`.

### 4.2 Object graphics and sprite drawing

`interactionData` (3 bytes per id, or a pointer to a list by subid) gives the object graphics header, the OAM tile base, the default animation, the palette and the VRAM bank. `interactionInitGraphics` loads the graphics through `objectGfxHeaderTable` into `wLoadedObjectGfx` (`$cc08` in Ages, `$cc07` in Seasons: eight 2-byte entries, header index and use mark, entry n loaded in VRAM at `$8000 + n x $200`, 32 tiles) and sets `oamTileIndexBase`; enemies and parts work the same way (`enemyData`, `partData`). An animation is a list of three-byte entries in the bank of its kind's animation table (`interactionAnimationTable`, `enemyAnimationTable`, `partAnimationTable`, one list of animations per id): the frames to hold the pose, the pose's index, `animParameter`; a duration of `$ff` is the loop marker, followed by the low byte of an offset (high byte `$ff`) added to the marker's next address, read once. `interactionAnimate` and its sisters count `animCounter` down and, at zero, read the entry at `animPointer`, move it past the entry, and set `oamDataAddress` to the word the index names in the kind's OAM data table for the object's id (its two high bits cut, `$40` added for an interaction and a part); the object's own code calls them, once or more a frame, and chooses the animation (`interactionSetAnimation`). The block `oamDataAddress` designates is a count followed by quadruplets Y, X, tile, attributes relative to the object's position. The view's step (`engine/neighbours/object_animation.c`) advances a captured object's animation by the same data, one call at each return of `updateAllObjects` in normal play (`wScrollMode` 1, no transition, text, menu, `wDisabledObjects` or palette fade), checked against the live game by `--object-animation-check`. An object is animated only when its own code advanced its animation by one call of the step at each of two updates of the objects the ghost runs after its capture (`ORACLES_GHOST_PROBE_UPDATES`, `ghost.h`): Vasu's snakes (`vasu.s`, state 1, set their animation again each frame Link is away), Tingle's balloon (`tingleBalloon.s`, no `partAnimate`) or a springbloom flower waiting in its state 1 (`springBloomFlower.s`) stay in their captured pose.

`drawAllSprites` (`00:0d9a`): `queueDrawEverything` calls `objectQueueDraw` (`00:10a8`) for each visible object, ranked in `wObjectsToDraw` (`$c500`) by priority, sixteen at most per level; then each object becomes 8x16 sprites in `wOam` (`drawAllSprites@drawObject`), placed by `_getObjectPositionOnScreen`, which subtracts the camera and the screen offset (`_getObjectPositionOnScreen_duringScreenTransition` during a scrolling transition, section 4.3). An object is drawn only if bit 7 of `visible` is set (bit 6: terrain effects). `@drawObject` writes into `wOam` (`$a0` bytes, forty entries) and skips each sprite whose OAM y byte, offset included, reaches `$a0` (line 144) or whose x byte reaches `$a8` (column 160): outside a transition, a sprite past the bottom or the right of the screen is not written, sprite by sprite. Outside a transition, a sprite is placed in y at `yh − hCameraY + $10 + zh` plus its offset, in x at `xh − hCameraX` plus its own; its tile is `oamTileIndexBase` plus the block's, its attributes `oamFlags` xor the block's; the block is in bank `BASE_OAM_DATA_BANK` (`$13` in Ages, `$12` in Seasons) plus the two high bits of `oamDataAddress`, at `(oamDataAddress | $4000) & $7fff`. An object on the ground whose tile underfoot is a puddle is drawn one pixel lower. Terrain effects (`_drawObjectTerrainEffects`, for any object with bit 6 of `visible`, none in a side-scrolling room): an object in the air queues a shadow every other frame, written after the loop of objects at its position without `zh`; an object on the ground, outside a scrolling transition, writes the grass or the puddle before its own sprites. `func_0eda` writes these blocks as they are, whole or not at all depending on the room left in the OAM.

A renderer that draws the objects from `wObjectsToDraw` or from the object table, with their OAM blocks, reproduces the image without the hardware limit of ten sprites per line; to equal the core's framebuffer it reads the OAM committed by DMA and applies the CGB PPU's selection of ten objects per line by OAM index.

### 4.3 Objects across a scrolling transition

1. Decision: in `updateAllObjects`, after the active room's enemies, parts and interactions are updated, `screenTransitionState2` writes `wScrollMode = $04` and `wScreenTransitionState = 3` (from an edge, which requires `w1Link.enabled`, or from bit 7 of `wScreenTransitionDirection`).
2. The same iteration, `cutscene01` calls `getNextActiveRoom`, which looks up `mapTransitionGroupTable` before the standard transition: the entered room is not always the grid's neighbour. The table, indexed by group, is a dictionary of room and case ended by a null room; it routes Seasons' Lost Woods (`0:40`) and the sword upgrade room (`0:c9`), Ages' nine rooms of the scrambled forest (`0:70` to `0:92`), and in group 5 Onox's dungeon and the eye puzzles. The Lost Woods send Link back to their entrance unless he follows the right sequence of directions, counted in `wLostWoodsTransitionCounter1` and `2`: the room reached from a room of the woods depends on the sequence and changes at every transition. Then `setObjectsEnabledTo2` moves every object of mode 1 to mode 2 (mode 3 persists), the tileset and layout load, `wScrollMode = $08`, and `initializeRoom` creates the entered room's objects: first those of the room's own code, Maple, a remembered companion and Ages' time portal, then `parseObjectData` in mode 1 in the free slots (sixteen per type, taken in order; an opcode without a slot is dropped), then `parseStaticObjects`. During the whole scroll the objects of both rooms share bank 1: what the room left occupies changes the slots, and possibly the number, of the objects created.
3. While `wScrollMode` is `$08` the objects are frozen, except as the table below says: the initialisation of a newly created object runs during the scroll, then it stays frozen, and a frozen object's animation does not advance. `drawAllSprites` draws them with a 16-bit offset (`hFF90`-`hFF93`) by mode (an object of mode 3 is placed by the camera) and drops a whole object whose position leaves a window.
4. End of the scroll: `wScrollMode` returns to 1; `finishScrollingTransition` moves Link back, resets the camera and calls `clearObjectsWithEnabled2`, which erases every object of mode 2: the room left has no objects any more.

No object of a room exists outside the active room and the transition in progress, except those of mode 3 and those `wStaticObjects` remembers: an entered room receives its objects in their initial state, computed at that moment.

| Type | Routine | Frozen when | Updated all the same |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| enemies | `updateEnemies` | `wScrollMode & $0e`, `wTextIsActive`, `wDisabledObjects & $84`, `wPaletteThread_mode` not zero | `state` and `substate` at 0 |
| interactions | `updateInteractions` | `wScrollMode = $08`, `wDisabledObjects & $02`, `wTextIsActive` | bit 7 of `enabled` set, or `state` at 0 |
| parts | `updateParts` | `wScrollMode = $08`, `wTextIsActive`, `wDisabledObjects & $88` | bit 7 of `enabled` set, or `state` at 0 |

Bit 7 of `wDisabledObjects` also freezes Link, the companion and the items.

What else a created object depends on:

- `obj_RandomEnemy` (`$f6`) draws positions from a buffer that `getRandomNumber` fills, and the RNG advances whenever any object draws a number: the RNG at the entry into a room depends on everything that ran before. On a scrolling transition, `checkPositionValidForEnemySpawn` refuses the three rows or columns of the edge Link enters by; on a warp, the cells within three tiles of the destination. So a randomly placed enemy's position depends on the edge of entry and on the RNG; near half the enemies of the object lists are placed this way.
- `checkEnemyKilled`: up to seven killable enemies per room receive an index; `wEnemiesKilledList` keeps, for the last eight rooms visited, the room number and the bitset of the enemies killed, which do not reappear; a warp's `loadingRoom` empties the list.
- `setEnemyTargetToLinkPosition` copies Link's position to `hEnemyTargetY/X` before `updateEnemies`: the enemies aim at Link through those bytes.
- The sprite tiles of an object depend on the order in which the room loaded its graphics (`wLoadedObjectGfx`, section 4.2).

## 5. Tile animation

`initializeAnimations` loads, for `wTilesetAnimation`, a group (`animationGroups.s`) that activates up to four animation streams (`wAnimationState` bits 0-3), each a list of pairs duration / graphics index with a loop (`animationData.s`, `docs/ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md`, section 10). Flowers, water and torches move by replacing tiles in VRAM, not by changing the layout.

Each frame, `updateAnimations` (`04:5906`) clears bit 6 of `wAnimationState`, takes a single index out of the queue (setting bit 6 if it takes one), then counts the active streams: a counter (`wAnimationCounterN`) followed by its pointer (`wAnimationPointerN`, three bytes per stream), the pointer designating the current entry's graphics index, the next byte the next entry's delay. At zero, the index is queued (`w2AnimationQueue`, 32 entries, `wAnimationQueueHead`/`Tail`; an index is lost when the queue is full) and the next delay read; a delay of `$ff` is the loop marker, followed by the low byte of an offset that, added to that byte's address with `$ff` as high byte, goes back to the start of the list. A copy's header (`animationGfxHeaders`, six bytes): ROM bank, source (high then low byte), destination (high then low, VRAM bank in bit 0), size in 16-byte blocks minus one. The copy reaches VRAM at the vblank after the frame that dequeues it, or the one after when the game's frame overruns. The view's reader (`engine/neighbours/animation.c`) starts from this state, read in the ghost for a neighbour, and advances it step for step with the game.

During a scroll between two rooms the animation is frozen and resumes where it stopped: `updateAnimations` returns before the queue and the counters while bit 0 of `wScrollMode` is clear (`$04` when the edge is touched, `$08` during the whole transition), and only `loadAnimationData` rewrites the counters, called at the transition when the entered room's `wTilesetAnimation` differs from `wLoadedTilesetAnimation`: the animation then restarts from its initial state. The frames of the scroll are never counted: across a scroll under the same animation, the game takes a single step between the last frame before the freeze and the first after. The entered room has loaded its tileset (`wTilesetAnimation`, `wTilesetGfx`) by the first frozen frame. Indices queued when the freeze starts stay queued and reach VRAM after it.

**Reading.** A hook reads freely, by physical address (bank and offset: `w3TileMappingData`, `w2BgPalettesBuffer`, `w4StatusBarTileMap`, `w7TextboxMap` and the object table share the same window `$d000-$dfff`), through direct access to the core's memory, never through the guest CPU's bus, which returns `$ff` on VRAM and OAM while the PPU draws and drops VRAM writes in mode 3. Reading or writing `SVBK`/`VBK` to reach a bank is a guest write, and forbidden.

**Writing.** Only between `drawAllSprites` and the `resumeThreadNextFrame` that follows, or in the events marked safe (after `initializeRoom`; the return of `getTextAddress`, whose answer alone a language replaces, wherever the game looks a text up; the tests of the swim's strokes, whose register alone the hold changes; the instant text's two tests, whose register alone it changes; the cheats' instructions, whose register alone a cheat changes; the compact text's four points in the text thread, which write the registers of a character it keeps, its line's graphics and buffers, its cursor's place and its reveal in the box's map, and its vblank, which writes the line's tiles in the VRAM); otherwise never during `updateAllObjects`. A hook never changes SP or the stacks, the threads' saved contexts included, nor the bank registers. The presentation writes nothing; the only writes to the live game are eight closed transactions of the guest bus (`engine/game/guest/`):

- **The continuous transitions** (`--continuous-transitions`) write at two points, three with `--continuous-steps`: at the return of `drawAllSprites`, and at the vblank of a frame the main loop did not finish while the transition loads the room (`wScreenTransitionState` 3 or 4: layout, collisions, unique graphics, whose code does not touch these fields); an unfinished frame of the scroll itself (state 5) is not written, its logic possibly standing in the middle of Link's own movement, and the harness counts the vblank writes by state (`transitions.vblank_scroll_writes` is zero by construction). The fields: `wcd14` (the step), `w1Link.x` and `w1Link.y`, `w1Link.animCounter`, `animParameter`, `animPointer` and `var31` (the walk), the two `wOam` entries of Link's sprite and, at the vblank, the same in the core's OAM, and `hDirtyBgPalettes` outside a transition. The bus checks the state itself (a scrolling transition of a small room, Link on foot, nothing carried). With `--continuous-steps` there is a third point, the **entry** of `drawAllSprites`, for one frame alone, the frame whose logic ended a scroll: back in normal play (`wScreenTransitionState` 2, `wScrollMode` 1) when the policy was last asked in state 5 (or a savestate's load gave back state 5, the policy's own record of it, else the state's RAM), Link in his normal state; `screenTransitionState5`'s last phase and `finishScrollingTransition` ran there after `linkState01` had skipped his move, and the game moves him again only at the next frame. The guest asks the policy at that entry and not at the return that follows; the fields are `w1Link.x` and `w1Link.y`, his step of that frame, nothing in `wOam` (`drawAllSprites` then draws him, and the grass or the puddle under him, which the return would have left a pixel behind him, where he is), his walk left to the game, which loaded that frame's pose already; and `wLinkLocalRespawnY` and `wLinkLocalRespawnX`, which `finishScrollingTransition` took from where the policy's steps had taken him, possibly far into the room: they go where the game's own scroll would have left him (its 15 or 16 px along the axis from where the transition began, on the straight path across), within the room: from 1 to the boundary past which the game starts a transition down or right (`wScreenTransitionBoundaryY/X`), as the game itself may leave him, along the axis 6 or more from the top and left edges where it puts him back, across where he stood (nearer, after a swim carried into a corner), so that a whirlpool, a hole or a drowning in the room entered brings him back to the place the game would have, never next to what threw him in (Ages and Seasons; the fan games' profiles do not name the field, and nothing is written there). In the scroll itself (states 3 to 5) the option adds `w1Link.direction` on foot, 0 to 3, the way `updateLinkDirectionFromAngle` would turn him to the direction held, which `loadLinkAndCompanionAnimationFrame` adds to his frame at the next frame and the game itself takes up after the scroll; `finishScrollingTransition` copies it into `wLinkLocalRespawnDir`, so that a fall or a drowning in the room entered brings him back facing the way he walked in. With `--continuous-swim` the bus also takes Link swimming at the surface in his normal state (bits 0-3 of `wLinkSwimmingState` at 3, bit 6 clear, diving or not; the `SWIM` or `DIVE` animation), and on Ages' sea floor, where the game has him walk (`LINK_ANIM_MODE_WALK`, `wLinkSwimmingState` zero, `var2f` bit 7): the same fields, the move bounded by a pixel an axis on foot and by `$160` (8.8, `SPEED_160` along an axis, the fastest swim) in the water; a side view (`TILESETFLAG_SIDESCROLL`) is never written. With `--continuous-diagonal` the move goes across the transition's axis too, within the same bounds, onto the room entered's collisions: at the vblanks of state 3, when `wRoomCollisions` may not be the room entered's yet, onto those the policy predicts from the ROM's tables and the bytes the load reads (`engine/gameplay/room_predict.c`, read, never written), in the rooms whose load is only those tables (not a room with its own code, nor a profile that does not qualify it: the fan games), and compared with what the game then loads at every transition (`transitions.predicted_mismatched`); without a prediction, never. `--continuous-swim` adds one field, at the return of `drawAllSprites` in normal play (`wScreenTransitionState` 2, `wScrollMode` 1), never at a vblank: bit 7 of `wScreenTransitionDirection`, raised to one of the four directions for the transition a stroke of the flippers carries Link to, or lowered when it was raised and not taken. `screenTransitionState2` reads it first and starts the transition without its other checks, which the bus makes itself: Link swimming at the surface in his normal state, never in a side view, at the edge of that direction, enabled, transitions neither disabled nor delayed, nothing in `wcc92`, the direction held none or square across the edge's axis.
- **The item hotkeys** (`--item-hotkeys`) write at one point: the return of `checkReloadStatusBarGraphics` from its only `call` in `mainThreadStart`, found in the user's ROM when the option arms (exactly one, else the option is refused; the tail jumps of `bank2.s` to the same function return elsewhere and do not count). It is the one moment when bit 0 of `wStatusBarNeedsRefresh` set by the host survives until the next turn's `updateStatusBar`, `checkReloadStatusBarGraphics` clearing the byte every turn. The fields: an exchange of two of the eighteen contiguous slots `wInventoryB`, `wInventoryA`, `wInventoryStorage[0..15]`, one of them a button, never a new value; `wSatchelSelectedSeeds`, `wShooterSelectedSeeds` (0 to 4, a seed obtained) and `wSelectedHarpSong` (Ages, 1 to 3, a song obtained); bit 0 of `wStatusBarNeedsRefresh` by an OR. The bus applies it only in the states where the player could open the inventory (`b2_updateMenus`), on an inventory where each item appears once.
- **The mods' calls** share that point and write no variable: at the instruction that returns from that same call (SP and return address equal to those of the entry, a conditional return taken), in a state without a lasting refusal of the inventory, the transaction pushes under the return address that of a routine from a closed list (`giveTreasure`, `loseTreasure`, `removeRupeeValue`, `playSound`, `setGlobalFlag`, `unsetGlobalFlag`) and sets `a` and `c`; the routine returns to the `call resumeThreadNextFrame`, which reads no register. It is the one exception to the rule on SP and the stacks, one call a frame. The item hotkeys use the same transaction for one sound, `playSound` with `snd_selectitem` (the inventory's equip sound in `bank2.s`, `@finalizeEquip`), queued at the start of a frame after an item they equipped or took into a slot. That call alone may also run with the inventory open: `mainThreadStart` goes on calling `checkReloadStatusBarGraphics` under the menu, and the menu plays its own sounds from the same thread, so the sound of an item taken from the inventory plays there at once instead of waiting for the menu to close.
- **A language** (`--language`) writes at one point: the instruction `getTextAddress` returns to, the return captured at its entry, in the text thread. A language composed into the image (`docs/ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md`, section 16) carries an index of its texts; when it has the language's text for `wTextIndexH:wTextIndexL` (a text's group, never the dictionary's four), the transaction writes that text's CPU address in `hl` and its bank in `a` and `w7ActiveBank`, the routine's whole answer, which its callers read next through `readByteFromW7ActiveBank` and keep in the text stack; a text the language keeps in English or lacks is left as the routine found it. An entry's address may carry bit 15, a text laid out again for the compact text (`docs/ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md`, section 8.4), which the transaction drops and counts; with the compact text, English (US) is composed and served the same way, its index naming only the texts laid out again. The language's texts lie each within one bank of the extension, decoded (no dictionary code), their control codes as the US engine reads them; nothing else is written, and without a language nothing is hooked.
- **The swim's hold** (`--swim-hold`) writes at two points and no memory, each the `and` that follows a stroke routine's `ld a,(wGameKeysJustPressed)`, found in the user's ROM within the routine's first `$40` bytes when the option arms (exactly once, else the option is refused), before it runs, with `d` on Link's object: `linkUpdateVelocity@mermaidSuit` (Ages), its mask the four directions, and `linkUpdateFlippersSpeed@flippersState0` (both games), its mask A. At the suit's, when a direction is held (`wGameKeysPressed`) that gives Link an angle (`wLinkAngle`'s bit 7 clear; two opposite directions give none), none was newly pressed, the stroke is spent or ending (`var3e`, decremented, would turn negative), and a stroke would serve (`speed` below `speedTmp`, `angle` not `wLinkAngle`, or `var12` a frame from `var13`), the transaction adds the directions held to `a`; at the flippers', when A, or a direction that gives an angle, is held and A is not newly pressed, it adds A, the routine being in its state 0, waiting for a stroke, so that a direction held strokes with the flippers as with the suit. Only the routine's next test reads `a` (past the flippers' test, both ways load it again): the game plays its stroke as for a press, its sound and its counters included. These and the cheats' are the only writes during `updateAllObjects`, a register's value at the one instruction that reads it, never a variable; a savestate taken with the option loads without it.
- **The instant text** (`--text-speed instant`, Ages and Seasons, US code, and the three fan games made on them, whose two routines are the US ones at other addresses; in either profile: it changes the game's pace, not what the core draws) writes at two points and no memory, in the text thread, both found in the user's ROM when it is first turned on (exactly once, else it is refused), before they run; once armed they stay so, and answer only while it is on, so that it changes from one frame to the next. The standard text (`wTextDisplayMode` 0) reveals a line a character at a time, `wTextSpeed`'s delay apart (7 to 2 frames, the speed of the file screen, kept in the save), and `updateCharacterDisplayTimer`, which states 2, 4 and `$a` call before each character and states 3 and 9 between two lines, is the one routine that reads A and B for the reveal: on a press it sets bit 0 of `w7d0c1` and returns at once, and `displayNextTextCharacter` loops on itself to the line's end in that frame (`jr nz,displayNextTextCharacter` after its `bit 0,a`), the next line shown whole too after its first delay, the bit cleared by the page's wait (state 5) on the A that turns the page. **At `updateCharacterDisplayTimer`'s `and BTN_A|BTN_B`** (within its first `$40` bytes), after `ld a,(wKeysJustPressed)`, the transaction adds A to `a` where neither A nor B is pressed: each line shows whole in the frame after `drawLineOfText` composes it, as a player pressing A at its start gets it, through the game's own path, its scroll, its arrow, its wait for A, its sound once a line (`@endLine`'s), its choices and their cursor's delay, its `\stop` and `\wait`. While `w7TextSlowdownTimer` runs (`\slow`, 120 frames: the essences' texts), the routine returns before the test: the game's lock holds. **At `displayNextTextCharacter`'s `bit 0,a`** (within its first `$60` bytes), after `ld e,<w7d0c1 / ld a,(de)`, when the character just written has a sound effect (`w7LineSoundEffectsBuffer` at `w7NextTextColumnToDisplay` less one, the voice of a Goron, a Tokay, Ricky, or the compact text's step's), the transaction takes bit 0 away from `a`: the loop stops on that character, `@readSubsequentLineBuffers` plays its sound and its voice, and the next frame's test starts the loop again; the loop past such a character, which a player's press makes, would leave the voice unplayed. Only these tests read `a` there. `wTextSpeed` and the save are never read or written; the bit 0 of `w7d0c1` the game sets is a press's, so that a savestate taken in the middle of a page loads with the option on or off, as after a press. The inventory's descriptions (`wTextDisplayMode` 2) and the choices' cursor (mode 1) do not go through these routines; without the option nothing is hooked.
- **The cheats** (`--cheats`, Ages and Seasons, US code) write at three points and no memory, each found in the user's ROM within its routine's first `$40` bytes when the cheat is first turned on (exactly once, else the cheat is refused), before it runs; a point once armed stays armed and answers only while its cheat is on, so that a cheat may change from one frame to the next. **No damage**: `linkApplyDamage`'s body in bank 6 (the bank-0 `linkApplyDamage` and `linkApplyDamage_b5` reach it with `d` on `w1Link`) begins `ld h,d / ld l,<damageToApply / ld a,(hl) / ld (hl),$00 / or a`; at the `or a`, with `d` on Link's object and `a` a damage (bit 7 set), `a` becomes 0: the routine skips its sum into `w1Link.health`, `wLinkHealth` does not go down and no death follows from a hit, while the knockback (`knockbackCounter`, halved by the Steadfast Ring), the sound, the red flash and the invincibility frames the hit set stay the game's. Every loss of health by a hit goes there: the enemies' and parts' collisions (`applyDamageToLink`), the spikes (`dealSpikeDamageToLink`), a fall into a hole, the water and the lava, the companions' hits copied to Link; the losses the story writes itself (`wLinkHealth` written by a script or an interaction: Ages' bomb-upgrade fairy, the Smog's penalty, Seasons' scripted death at the Moblins' keep) do not, and stay. **Ammo never runs out**: `decNumBombs` shares its end with `decNumActiveSeeds` and `decNumBombchus`, `ld a,(hl) / or a / ret z / call setStatusBarNeedsRefreshBit1 / ld a,(hl) / sub $01 / daa / ld (hl),a / or h / ret`; at the `ld (hl),a`, `a` becomes the count `(hl)` holds, read by physical address: the count is written back as it was and `or h` answers as for a decrement; at zero the routine returns before, so that no ammunition is made. The one loss of ammunition the story writes through it, Ages' bomb-upgrade fairy taking every bomb from a Link who claimed the silver bomb (`bombUpgradeFairy_loseAllBombs` writes 1 and calls `decNumBombs`), is left to the game: that call, found in the routine in the ROM, its return address under `SP` and its bank mapped. **Rupees never run out**: `removeRupeeValue` is `ld hl,wNumRupees / call getRupeeValue / jp subDecimalFromHlRef`, Seasons' `removeOreChunkValue` jumping to its call with `hl` on `wNumOreChunks`; at the call, with `hl` on `wNumRupees`, the rupee value in `a` becomes 0 (`RUPEEVAL_000`, whose amount the cheat checks is none in the ROM's table when it arms): nothing is taken away. The ore chunks are left as they are, and so is a call of the mods' call transaction (the word under `SP` its write point), so that a mod's `game.pay` still charges. `cpRupeeValue`, the shops' test of the price, is not touched: with too few rupees nothing is bought.
- **The compact text** (`--text compact`, Ages and Seasons, US code, and the three fan games made on them, whose four routines are the US ones at other addresses, their calls and jumps aside, which the test on the user's ROMs checks with the WRAM they use; in Enhanced alone: it changes what the core draws, which Faithful shows as it is) writes registers and WRAM bank 7, in the text thread, at four points, the last two found in the user's ROM within their routine's first `$40` bytes when it arms (exactly once, else it is refused). `drawLineOfText` composes a line whole in one call: for each character, `setLineTextBuffers` keeps its character, attribute, delay and sounds at column `e` of the line's sixteen-byte buffers and moves `e` on, and `retrieveTextCharacter` draws its 8x16 cell, in the colour `\col` chose, at `bc`, two tiles further each time, in `w7TextGfxBuffer`, which `dmaTextGfxBuffer` queues for the VRAM after the call; the names, the numbers, the symbols and a choice's cursor place go through the same two routines, `bc` unchanged between them. **At `setLineTextBuffers`' entry**, within a line, the transaction keeps the character before as the game wrote its buffers and drew its cell, then gives `de` and `bc` the line's last column and cell for this one: the game's buffers never see more than sixteen characters, which lets a line the compact text lays out again hold more (`docs/ROM_DATA_FORMATS.md`, section 8.4). **At `drawLineOfText`'s return**, it composes the 512 bytes of `w7TextGfxBuffer` again from the cells it kept: each character's columns of ink at the pen, which moves by the character's width and a column, a blank character by 5 pixels, and a character whose ink reaches its cell's right edge by its width alone, the next one joining it when its own ink starts its cell (the A and B buttons, each drawn across two characters, `$b8 $b9` and `$ba $bb`); a symbol (`$06`, an icon or a kanji), a secret's character (`\secret`, which `@controlCodeA` prints from `w7SecretText1` or 2: `setLineTextBuffers` receives it with `hl` past it there) and a choice's cursor place take a whole tile from the next tile's edge, the secret drawn as the game draws it, for the player to copy, and a character with ink starts the next tile when the tile it would share holds ink of another attribute (a tile has one palette); a character the line's 128 pixels do not hold is not drawn, and counted. It then writes the line's buffers as the reveal's steps: a character a step, sixteen at most, a blank character joining the step before it, then the two narrowest neighbours, when the line holds more; a step's delay is the sum of its characters' delays, so that the dialogue keeps its characters per second, its sound its first character's that is not a space (none for a step of spaces), its sound effect its first character's, and its character `$80` plus the pixel its next step's first character starts at (for the last, the line's drawn pixels rounded up to its tile columns, 127 at most), which the game reads only as not zero and not a space; the attributes buffer holds each tile column's attribute: its ink's, else a blank character's on it, else the column's before it. The widths are read from the font of the image the core runs (`gfx_font_start`, a European language's glyphs included) when the transaction arms. **At `textControlCodeC_2`'s `ld (hl),b`**, `b` holds the cursor's place `(e * 2 | $60) + 1` the routine keeps in `w7TextboxOptionPositions`, which `updateSelectedTextPosition` reads back to draw the cursor and `removeCursorFromSelectedTextPosition` to put the tile back: its column bits become the tile column the place will start at, the next tile's edge after the characters before it, and the space the routine prints next takes that whole tile. **At `displayNextTextCharacter`'s `ld d,>w7NextTextColumnToDisplay`**, the routine having written step k's two tiles and attribute in `w7TextboxMap` and `w7TextboxAttributes` (`(w7d0cc + 2 + k) & $1f`, the top line's rows from 0, the bottom's from `$40`), the line's columns are written again up to k and up to the last its characters reach: the tile columns step k's characters reach, which its character says, with their tiles `w7d0d3 + 2j`, `+ 1` and their attribute, the others with the blank of the box's margin, its column `w7d0cc + 1`. **At the vblank** (the core's hook, after the hosts that read the frame just scanned, before the game's vblank handler copies the map the text thread wrote), while the standard text (`wTextDisplayMode` 0) reveals the line (`w7TextDisplayState` 2, 4 or `$a`, `w7NextTextColumnToDisplay` steps shown), the transaction writes the line's tile columns in the VRAM, bank 0, `$9400` the top line's and `$9600` the bottom's (`w7d0d3` `$40` or `$60`): those whole once the last step shown is, from `w7TextGfxBuffer`, and the one the next step starts in with only its pixels before that step, the others blank (colour 3, as `clearTextGfxBuffer` leaves them); once the line is whole (states 3, 5, `$b`, `$f`, or the choice that follows it, mode 1), its columns are written whole again, the bytes `dmaTextGfxBuffer` copied, but for a choice shown (mode 1) the tile columns of its cursor places (`w7TextboxOptionPositions`), blank in the line's graphics: the fan games' engine (hack-base's) draws the cursor in those tiles of the VRAM (`updateSelectedTextPosition`, `removeCursorFromSelectedTextPosition`, through `retrieveTextCharacter` and a DMA) where the US one writes its tile in the map. The next image shows the map and those tiles together: every character shown whole, none before its step, the game revealing a step at a time at its own pace, its sounds and its characters per second. The writes are a function of the WRAM alone, and no bytes of the line's tiles but those `dmaTextGfxBuffer` copied or a part of them; outside a text being revealed or just revealed nothing is written. What the reveal needs is in the line's buffers; only the characters of the line being composed, and where its call returns, are the host's while `drawLineOfText` runs: a savestate taken within the call (a vblank falling between two of its characters) carries them (`oracles_guest_compact_save_state`, the state's last field), and its load gives them back with the call's return, so that the line goes on and is composed there as if no load had come between. **The wide text box** (`--text-box wide`) arms the same four points and the same vblank, with the host's text box below: the transaction lays each line out at the box's 192 pixels rather than 128, in the font's widths with the compact text, or at the game's fixed advance with the classic text (each character a whole cell of eight pixels, a blank one drawing nothing: the game's own layout, past its sixteen characters), and groups its steps over the whole line; `w7TextGfxBuffer` receives the line's first 128 pixels, the steps' bytes their boundaries within them (127 at most), and the host's line the whole of it, its steps' boundaries on 16 bits. The game's buffers, its map and its VRAM are written as above for those first 128 pixels; the host draws the box from its own line. The inventory's descriptions (`wTextDisplayMode` 2), which do not go through `drawLineOfText`, keep the game's fixed width; without the option nothing is hooked.

**The host's text box** (Ages and Seasons, US code, armed only for a box the game cannot show, the wide box or three lines, at either size; never for the standard box of two lines, whose text drawn twice as large is the core's pixels scaled, so that the classic text in it keeps nothing hooked; and by the harness's `--text-box-check`, which compares the standard box it draws with the core's image) writes nothing: it keeps the box's lines on the host's side, as the VRAM holds them, to draw the box itself (`engine/enhanced/text_box.c`). At `drawLineOfText`'s return it keeps the line composed: with the compact text, the transaction's composition at the box's width; without it, the characters of `w7LineTextBuffer`, their attributes and the cells the game drew in `w7TextGfxBuffer`, laid out again at a fixed advance of a cell each, which is the game's own layout. `dmaTextGfxBuffer` adds `$94` to `a` (0 the top line, 2 the bottom) and queues the copy of `w7TextGfxBuffer` through `queueDmaTransfer`, which writes an entry of `wVBlankFunctionQueue` (`vblankDmaFunction`'s offset, the source's WRAM bank, its address, the VRAM bank, the destination, the length) with the LCD on, or copies at once with it off; the vblank's handler runs the queue only when the main loop waits for it (`wVBlankChecker`), each function entered with the entry's next byte pushed. At `vblankDmaFunction`'s entry the host reads that entry under `SP`, and an entry from `w7TextGfxBuffer` to `$9400` or `$9600` in VRAM bank 0, 512 bytes, copies the line composed into the host's top or bottom line, at the moment the DMA writes the VRAM; at `dmaTextGfxBuffer`'s entry, with the LCD off, the same at once. The compact text's reveal writes at the vblank are made in the host's line as in the VRAM, and at each vblank, before its writes, the lines are kept as the frame just scanned showed them. The host's lines keep their layout too: their characters (as `setLineTextBuffers` received them, whole or not, their attribute, their delay, their place on the line) and their steps, step s's characters ending before a pixel on 16 bits. In the game's box the compact text's reveal writes at the vblank are made in the host's line as in the VRAM; in the wide box the host's lines stay whole, and the reveal is read in the text's state, the game's map showing a line's first 128 pixels alone: at each vblank, after the frame's logic, while the standard text reveals a line (`wTextDisplayMode` 0, `w7TextDisplayState` 2, 4 or `$a`), `w7d0d3` says which, `w7NextTextColumnToDisplay` how many of its steps are shown (none past a step whose byte of `w7LineTextBuffer` is zero), and `w7LineDelaysBuffer`'s delay of the last one shown less `w7CharacterDisplayTimer` the frames since it showed; kept one vblank more, it is the reveal of the frame just scanned, the frame that shows the map that vblank copied. The box shows that step's characters one after the other, each once the delays of those before it have passed, never past the step's boundary, every other line whole. At `drawLineOfText`'s entry, in the wide box, the host reads where the line starts, `w7TextAddress` in bank `w7ActiveBank`: a line outside the pages the composition laid out again (their places in the image, given to the host when it arms) is of a page the game laid out for its 128 pixels, and the host's line starts 32 pixels further, centred, its characters, steps, a choice's cursor and its icons with it; the game's buffers and VRAM keep the line where the game draws it. These lines and the reveal outlive a frame: the composite savestate carries them, its field `text box state` after the transitions' state (`engine/core/state.h`), empty without the host's text box and read as empty from the states written before it (format 2: the format, the box's tile columns, the reveal, each line's flag, columns, attributes, tiles, characters and steps; a field of another format or width is refused, the lines then unknown until the game copies a line again); a load forgets them, then reads the field.

The ghost instance, a disposable copy of the core, runs the same image and serves a language as the live instance does; it is the only other one to receive writes: bit 7 of `wScreenTransitionDirection` and `wDisableWarpTiles` to prime it, Link's position at the edge, `wRoomStateModifier` (Seasons, the season held) and, at the entry of `checkRoomPack`, `wRoomPack`.

What the renderer reads each frame: `wActiveGroup`, `wActiveRoom`, `wRoomIsLarge`, `hCameraX/Y`, `wScreenOffsetX/Y`, `wScreenShakeCounterX/Y`, `wRoomLayout`, `w3TileMappingData` and the attributes, `w2BgPalettesBuffer`/`w2SprPalettesBuffer`, `wAnimationState` and the pointers, `wObjectsToDraw` or the object table with their OAM blocks, `w4StatusBarTileMap`, `wTextIsActive` and the text box (`w7TextboxMap`). A neighbouring room's terrain never comes from the ROM with reimplemented substitutions: it comes from a ghost instance in which the game loads the room itself.

## 7. Seasons

The same engine, structures and threads, not the same addresses: `hram.s` and `wram.s` have conditional blocks by game (`hCameraX` is `$ffac` in Ages and `$ffaa` in Seasons; `wActiveGroup` `$cc2d` against `$cc49`; `wGlobalFlags` `$c6d0` against `$c6ca`), which is why every address is resolved per game from the generated tables. Other differences: the code banks of interactions and enemies, the scripts in bank `$14`, group 1 is Subrosia (11x8 rooms), the tilesets change with the season through `wRoomStateModifier` and the room packs, eight seed trees instead of sixteen, a few special transitions (the Lost Woods, Onox). The disassembly marks the differences with `ROM_AGES`/`ROM_SEASONS` and `AGES_ENGINE`.
